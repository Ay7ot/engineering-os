# Copywriting

## When to use
- Drafting new page copy for a homepage, landing, pricing, feature or about page.
- Positioning a headline, subheadline or call to action.
- Rewriting copy that is accurate but not converting.

## Before writing
- Look for a project marketing context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`, or another path named in AGENTS.md) and `read` it before asking questions. If none exists, gather the same context and offer to create one there.
- Gather the page purpose and primary action; the audience, their problem, objections and the language they use; the offer, differentiators, transformation and proof points; the traffic source and what visitors already know.

## Principles
- Clarity over cleverness. When clear and creative conflict, choose clear.
- Benefits over features. Features are what it does; benefits are what that means for the customer.
- Specificity over vagueness. "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes", not "Save time on your workflow".
- Customer language over company language. Mirror voice-of-customer from reviews, interviews and support tickets.
- One idea per section. Each section advances one argument and the page builds a logical flow.
- Show over tell. Describe the outcome instead of reaching for adverbs.

## Style rules
- Simple over complex. "Use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate".
- Specific over vague. Avoid "streamline", "optimize", "innovative".
- Active over passive. "We generate reports", not "Reports are generated".
- Confident over qualified. Cut "almost", "very", "really".
- Honest over sensational. Never fabricate statistics or testimonials.

## Page structure
- Above the fold: headline (the single most important message), subheadline (1-2 sentences adding specificity) and one primary CTA.
- Headline formulas: "{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}"; "The {category} for {audience}"; "Never {unpleasant event} again"; "{Question naming the main pain point}".
- Core sections in order: social proof; problem or pain; solution and benefits (3-5 key benefits); how it works (3-4 steps); objection handling (FAQ, comparisons, guarantees); final CTA (recap value, risk reversal).

## CTA copy
- Weak, avoid: Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Click Here, Get Started.
- Strong: Start Free Trial, Get [specific thing], See [product] in Action, Create Your First [thing], Download the Guide.
- Formula: action verb plus what they get plus a qualifier if needed. Example: "Start My Free Trial".

## Page-specific
- Homepage: serve several audiences without going generic; lead with the broadest value proposition and give clear paths per intent.
- Landing page: one message, one CTA; match the headline to the ad or traffic source; complete the argument on one page.
- Pricing: help visitors choose; answer "which plan is right for me?"; make the recommended plan obvious.
- Feature: connect feature to benefit to outcome, with use cases and a clear path to try or buy.
- About: tell the story of why you exist, connected to customer benefit, still ending in a CTA.

## Voice and tone
Set formality (casual, professional but friendly, formal), brand personality (playful or serious, bold or understated, technical or accessible), and keep it consistent. Headlines can be bolder, body copy clearer, CTAs action-oriented.

## Checklist
- [ ] Marketing context read or gathered, not re-asked
- [ ] Headline names a specific outcome; subheadline adds specificity
- [ ] Benefits not a feature list; each claim answers "so what?"
- [ ] One primary CTA written as action plus outcome
- [ ] No fabricated statistics or testimonials
- [ ] Each section advances exactly one argument
