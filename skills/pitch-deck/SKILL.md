# Pitch Deck

## When to use
- A founder is preparing a deck for a round from pre-seed to Series A.
- An existing deck needs structural or narrative feedback.
- The question is what slides to include, or how to tell the story to investors.
- A deck keeps getting passed on after a first meeting.

## Context required
Pull from the shared context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`,
see `product-marketing-context`): one-liner, stage, product, target customer,
business model, traction, team and competition. Ask the founder for anything
missing, plus the target round size, investor type, and whether the deck is for
a live pitch (visual, 30 to 40 words per slide) or a send-ahead (60 to 80 words
per slide, readable alone).

## Workflow
1. Read the context and list the gaps before drafting a single slide.
2. Decide live pitch or send-ahead; it changes how much text each slide carries.
3. Draft the narrative arc in prose first: today's world, what your product changes, why now, why this team, what the money buys.
4. Write slides one by one. Each gets a title, the one message to remember, supporting points and a suggested visual.
5. Review every slide against the question an investor would ask at that point and flag weak spots.
6. Deliver the deck as structured markdown, one heading per slide. Only convert to presentation format after the content is settled.

## The 10 to 12 slides
| # | Slide | Purpose | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title and hook | Name, one-liner, a memorable stat | Generic tagline, buried one-liner |
| 2 | Problem | Make the pain specific to the ICP | Abstract, or a problem everyone already knows |
| 3 | Solution | How you remove the pain | Feature dump, no link back to the problem |
| 4 | Demo or product | Screenshot, clip or walkthrough | Showing the admin panel, not the magic |
| 5 | Market size | TAM, SAM, SOM with bottoms-up maths | Top-down "1 percent of $X trillion" |
| 6 | Business model | How you make money, unit economics | No real or projected unit economics |
| 7 | Traction | The chart that goes up | Vanity metrics, hidden axis, mixed timeframes |
| 8 | Competition | Why you win | "No competitors", or a 2x2 you always win |
| 9 | Team | Founders and founder-market fit | Listing everyone, no reason this team |
| 10 | Go to market | How you acquire customers now and at scale | "We will go viral" with no channel |
| 11 | Financials and ask | Raise, use of funds, milestones | No milestone the money unlocks |
| 12 | Closing vision | Where this goes in 5 to 10 years | Too conservative, no contact details |

## Narrative arc
- Slides 1 to 3 establish tension. The problem must land before the answer.
- Slides 4 to 7 build credibility: real product, real traction, real market.
- Slides 8 to 10 prove defensibility against alternatives and show scaling.
- Slides 11 to 12 make the ask and end on ambition, not logistics.

## Stage guidance
- Pre-seed and seed: emphasise problem depth, founder-market fit and early signals such as waitlists, letters of intent and design partners. Lighter financials are acceptable.
- Series A: emphasise a repeatable go-to-market, unit economics and a path from current traction to 3 to 5x growth. Investors expect real revenue.

## Reviewing a deck
Read the deck, mark which of the slides above are missing or weak, test the
narrative arc for tension, and check each slide against its investor question.
Return specific rewrites, not general advice.

## Principles
- Every slide title is an assertion, not a label. "We grew 30 percent month on month for six months" beats "Traction".
- Cut any slide that does not advance the narrative. If you cannot justify it, remove it.
- It must be understandable by a partner reading it alone at night.
- Use at most three fonts and two brand colours.
- Data beats adjectives. Replace "fast-growing" with "3x year on year".

## Checklist
- [ ] Context gaps listed and filled before drafting
- [ ] Live pitch or send-ahead decided, text density matches
- [ ] Narrative arc written before slides
- [ ] Every slide title is an assertion
- [ ] Each slide answers its investor question
- [ ] Round size, use of funds and unlocked milestones stated
