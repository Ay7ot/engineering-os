# Copy Editing

## When to use
- Polishing a draft, tightening wordy copy, or fixing sentences that read awkwardly.
- Reviewing copy before it ships ("review my copy", "copy feedback").
- Refreshing outdated pages: traffic declining, statistics stale, product changed.

## Rules
- Do not change the core message; enhance it. Preserve the author's voice while improving clarity.
- Multiple focused passes beat one unfocused review. Every edit needs a clear reason.
- Propose a specific fix, not just a complaint, and let the author make the final call.
- Read the project marketing context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`) first for brand voice and customer language.

## The seven sweeps
Run in order. After each sweep, loop back so earlier ones still hold.
1. Clarity: confusing structures, unclear pronoun references, jargon, ambiguity, missing context. Note problems before proposing edits; confirm one main idea per section and that the copy speaks to the reader.
2. Voice and tone: formal/casual shifts, inconsistent brand personality, jarring mood, off-brand words. Read aloud to hear them.
3. So what: features without benefits, claims without consequences. Ask "so what?" after each claim and add the benefit bridge, for example "AI-powered analytics" becomes "AI-powered analytics surface insights you would miss manually, so you decide in half the time".
4. Prove it: unsubstantiated claims and "best"/"leading" with no evidence. Prefer named testimonials, case references, statistics, third-party validation, guarantees, logos and review scores.
5. Specificity: vague words ("improve", "enhance", "optimize"), generic statements, suspiciously round numbers. Add numbers, timeframes or examples; delete what cannot be made specific.
6. Heightened emotion: flat language, pain points stated but not felt. Paint the before state, use sensory language, micro-stories and reflection questions. Emotion must serve the message, never manipulate.
7. Zero risk: friction near CTAs, unanswered objections, missing trust signals, hidden costs. Add guarantees, free trials, "no credit card required", "cancel anytime", proof near the CTA and clear next steps.

## Quick-pass checks
Use when a full seven-sweep review is overkill.
- Cut: very, really, extremely, incredibly; just, actually, basically; in order to (use "to"); unnecessary "that"; things, stuff.
- Replace: utilize to use, implement to set up, leverage to use, facilitate to help, innovative to new, robust to strong, seamless to smooth, cutting-edge to new.
- Watch: adverbs, passive voice, nominalizations ("make a decision" to "decide").
- Sentences: one idea each, varied length, front-load the important part, at most 3 conjunctions, usually under 25 words.
- Paragraphs: one topic, 2-4 sentences for web, strong opening line, white space for scanning.

## Expert panel
- For launch copy, pricing pages and high-traffic landing pages, assemble 3-5 expert personas relevant to the copy type (conversion copywriter, UX writer, target customer, brand strategist; add a deliverability specialist for email and a skeptical buyer plus editor for long-form).
- Each persona scores 1-10 and gives specific critiques. Revise the lowest scores first and re-score until every persona is 7+ and the panel averages 8+.
- Skip for quick updates and low-stakes content.

## Common problems and fixes
- Wall of features: add "which means..." after each feature.
- Corporate speak: ask "how would a human say this?".
- Weak opening: lead with the reader's problem or desired outcome.
- Buried CTA: make the ask obvious, early and repeated.
- No proof: add specific testimonials, numbers or case references.
- Generic claims: specify who, how and by how much.
- Mixed audiences: pick one audience and write directly to them.
- Feature overload: focus on the 3-5 benefits that matter most.

## Content refresh
When traffic declines, data is stale, or the product changed: check dates and statistics, re-verify every claim and link, re-align to current positioning, update examples and re-check search intent. Rewrite only when the angle is wrong, not merely when the facts are stale.

## Checklist
- [ ] Core message preserved; author's voice intact
- [ ] All seven sweeps run, and earlier sweeps re-checked after later edits
- [ ] Every claim has proof or is softened
- [ ] Vague words replaced with numbers, timeframes or examples
- [ ] Objections answered and risk reduced near the CTA
- [ ] High-stakes copy scored 7+ by every panel persona
