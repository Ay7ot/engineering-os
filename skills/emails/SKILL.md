# Email Sequence Design

## When to use
- Creating or optimizing an automated email sequence, drip campaign or lifecycle programme.
- Choosing sequence length, timing and triggers.
- Writing welcome, nurture, re-engagement, onboarding, billing, win-back or campaign emails.

## Before designing
- Read the project marketing context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`) first.
- Sequence type: welcome or onboarding, lead nurture, re-engagement, post-purchase, event-based, educational, or sales.
- Audience: who they are, what triggered entry, what they already know, and their current relationship with you.
- Goals: the primary conversion, relationship building, segmentation, and what defines success.

## Principles
- One email, one job: one primary purpose and one main CTA per email.
- Value before ask: lead with usefulness, build trust, then earn the right to sell.
- Relevance over volume: fewer, better emails; segment for relevance.
- Clear path forward: every email moves the reader somewhere and links somewhere useful.

## Length and timing
- Lengths: welcome 3-7 emails; lead nurture 5-10; onboarding 5-10; re-engagement 3-5. Tune to the sales-cycle length, product complexity and relationship stage.
- Timing: welcome immediately; early sequence 1-2 days apart; nurture 2-4 days; long-term weekly or bi-weekly. Avoid weekends for B2B, test them for B2C, and send at local time.
- Subject lines: clear over clever, specific over vague, benefit or curiosity led, 40-60 characters. Patterns: "Still struggling with X?", "How to [outcome] in [timeframe]", "3 ways to [benefit]", "[First name], your [thing] is ready", "The mistake I made with [topic]".
- Preview text: about 90-140 characters, extends the subject line rather than repeating it.

## Sequence shapes
- Welcome (5-7 emails over 12-14 days): welcome and deliver promised value, quick win, story and why, social proof, overcome an objection, core feature highlight, conversion.
- Lead nurture (6-8 over 2-3 weeks): deliver the lead magnet and intro, expand the topic, problem deep-dive, solution framework, case study, differentiation, objection handler, direct offer.
- Re-engagement (3-4 over 2 weeks, triggered by 30-60 days inactive): genuine check-in, what is new, incentive, last chance to stay or unsubscribe.
- Onboarding (5-7 over 14 days): welcome and first step, getting-started help, feature highlight, success story, check-in, advanced tip, upgrade.

## Email copy
- Structure: hook, context, value, CTA, human sign-off.
- Format for mobile-first reading: paragraphs of 1-3 sentences, white space, bullets for scanning, bold sparingly.
- Tone: conversational, first and second person, active voice; read it aloud and check it sounds human.
- Length: 50-125 words transactional, 150-300 educational, 300-500 story-driven.
- CTA: one clear primary action per email; buttons for primary actions, links for secondary; button text is action plus outcome.

## Email types to consider
- Onboarding: new user or customer series, key step reminders, invites.
- Retention: upgrade to paid or a higher plan, ask for a review, proactive support, usage reports, NPS survey, referral programme.
- Billing: switch to annual, failed-payment recovery, cancellation survey, upcoming renewal reminders.
- Usage: daily, weekly or monthly summaries, key event notifications, milestone celebrations.
- Win-back: expired trials, cancelled customers. Campaign: newsletter roundup, seasonal promotions, product and pricing updates.

## Output
- Sequence overview: name, trigger, goal, length, timing, exit conditions.
- Each email: purpose, send timing, subject, preview, body, CTA text and destination, and segment conditions.
- A metrics plan naming what to measure.

## Checklist
- [ ] Trigger, goal and exit conditions defined
- [ ] Length and cadence match the sales cycle and relationship stage
- [ ] One job and one primary CTA per email
- [ ] Subject and preview within length and not repeating each other
- [ ] Email supports in-app onboarding rather than duplicating it
- [ ] Metrics plan defined
