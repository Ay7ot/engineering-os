# YC and Sequoia Pitch Deck

## When to use
- Preparing a YC application narrative or a YC-format investor deck.
- Building or rehearsing a two-minute Demo Day pitch.
- Reviewing a deck against the YC and Sequoia standards rather than a general narrative.

## The method
The YC and Sequoia formats are the same story in a fixed order:
problem, solution, why now, market, product, traction, business model,
competition, team, ask. Every slide must earn its place. Investors see
thousands of decks and give you about three minutes, so a weak deck is rejected
fast even when the company is good. A great deck does not get you funded on its
own, so pair it with `fundraising` and `founder-sales`.

## Core slides, in order
| # | Slide | Purpose | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title | Who you are, what you do | 5 sec |
| 2 | Problem | The pain, specific | 30 sec |
| 3 | Solution | How you remove it | 30 sec |
| 4 | Why now | Market timing | 20 sec |
| 5 | Market size | TAM, SAM, SOM | 20 sec |
| 6 | Product | Demo or screenshots | 45 sec |
| 7 | Traction | Proof it works | 30 sec |
| 8 | Business model | How you make money | 20 sec |
| 9 | Competition | Why you win | 20 sec |
| 10 | Team | Why you are the ones | 20 sec |
| 11 | Ask | What you need and why | 20 sec |

Optional, only when the story needs it: go to market, roadmap, unit economics,
vision, financials (Series A and later).

## Leave out
Detailed projections too early, architecture unless deep tech, an advisory board
unless remarkable, pending patents, and long text blocks. Nobody reads them.

## Rules per slide
- Title: company name, a plain one-liner and contact. Never open with "We are" or jargon.
- Problem: who has it, what it is, what it costs. Use one customer story, quantify the pain, do not describe the solution yet.
- Solution: benefit first, one sentence, plain words. Show, do not tell.
- Why now: a technology, regulatory, behavioural or cost shift, and why not five years ago. "It is just time" is not a reason.
- Market size: bottom-up customer count times price. SAM and SOM matter more than TAM. A believable path to $100M or more.
- Product: the core experience and what is different. Pre-product: show the prototype and say it is not built yet.
- Traction: growth rate beats absolute numbers, retention beats new users, revenue beats free users, engagement beats signups. No traction: waitlist conversion, letters of intent, pilot commitments.
- Business model: revenue model, price per unit, and unit economics when available. An LTV to CAC ratio of 3 or better is the bar.
- Competition: never say "no competition". Use a 2x2 with axes you win on, or a plain why-we-win argument. Include the do-nothing alternative.
- Team: founders, relevant background, complementarity, founder-market fit. Weak team: name the advisers or the hires you will make, and be honest about the gap.
- Ask: the amount, a clear use of funds split and the milestones it unlocks. Avoid wide ranges such as "$1 to 5M".

## Traction by stage
- Pre-seed: waitlist, letters of intent, pilot customers.
- Seed: revenue, users, growth rate.
- Series A: revenue growth, retention, unit economics.

## Adjustments by stage
- Pre-seed, roughly $500K to $1M: problem, solution, team and early signal. Revenue and full CAC to LTV are not expected.
- Seed, roughly $1M to $3M: traction, a product-market fit signal and a go-to-market hypothesis. Some revenue or strong engagement expected.
- Series A, roughly $5M to $15M: unit economics, scalable go-to-market, repeatable sales. Expect $1M or more ARR and a proven CAC to LTV.

## The two-minute Demo Day pitch
Budget the time: hook 10 seconds, problem 20, solution 20, traction 20,
business model 15, market and why now 15, ask 10, close 10. Rehearse until the
flow is natural, pause for effect, memorise the structure rather than the exact
words, and end with energy and a visible contact.

## Reviewing a deck
Check the order, that each slide carries one point, that the problem is
quantified, the market is bottom-up, the traction is the best available, the
competition is honest and the ask is specific. Full per-slide do and do-not
lists, worked slide examples and a complete Demo Day script are in
`references/slide-templates.md`.

## Checklist
- [ ] Core slides present in the canonical order
- [ ] 10 to 15 slides maximum, each with one point
- [ ] Problem quantified with a specific customer
- [ ] Market sized bottom-up, SAM and SOM explicit
- [ ] Competition honest, "no competition" removed
- [ ] Ask names the amount, use of funds and unlocked milestones
- [ ] Two-minute script timed and rehearsed
