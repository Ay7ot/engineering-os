# Brand Kit Boards

One image that argues the brand world: identity decks, logo concept boards, visual-system presentations. The bar is a senior designer's presentation board, not a logo dump. `high-end-visual-design` and `better-colors` cover interface polish and palette contrast once the direction is set.

## When to use
- A brand-kit overview image, identity deck, logo concept board or visual-world poster.
- Showing one logo across UI, print, image and system detail in a single board.

## Strategy before pixels
Write one line for each before prompting: category, audience, product function, emotional promise, cultural position, trust level, visual world, the core metaphor, and what the brand must avoid. Every board answers: what does the brand represent, what is the metaphor, how does the logo express it, how does the system scale, why does it feel ownable.

Pick symbols from meaning, never at random: developer tool maps to cursor, frame, scaffold, grid; AI assistant to spark, orbit, node, path; security to shield, eye, boundary, protected core; gaming to dice, gem, trophy; voice to waveform, orb, pulse; compliance to seal, badge, document; drone or robotics to wing, crosshair, zone; luxury to monogram, seal, emboss; productivity to path, check, block.

## Logo standard
Simple, memorable, symbolic, scalable, ownable, balanced, and usable as icon, wordmark, badge and pattern. Use one concept method, or two at most:
- Monogram plus meaning: the initial cut, folded or built from geometry, not a plain letter.
- Product action: build maps to frame, protect to shield, convert to switch, speak to wave.
- Metaphor fusion: owl plus drone vision, shield plus mountain, dice plus economy.
- Negative space: hidden arrow, cutout initial, an eye formed by crossing shapes.
- Construction geometry: circles, diagonals, grids, orbital paths, measured linework.

Avoid generic bolts, random animals, fake crests, copied marks, clipart, meaningless sparkles and inconsistent variants.

## Compose the board
Default is a 3 by 3 grid at 4:3 or 16:10 on a clean presentation canvas with strong gutters and generous negative space. Other layouts: 2 by 3 deck, 2 by 2 concept, 1 by 3 strip, 4 by 2 contact sheet. Panel roles, in order: logo cover; logo construction; digital application (browser chrome, app icon, terminal); brand essence tagline; colour system; typography specimen; physical application (card, badge, label); image direction; system detail (chips, icon row, command line). Give the board rhythm: quiet, functional, emotional, technical, atmospheric, detailed. Never make every panel equally loud. The eight visual modes, each with its palette and logo logic, are in references/visual-modes.md.

## Text, colour, images
- Very little text: brand name, one tagline, one URL, one command, 2 to 5 labels, short chips. No fake body copy, no lorem ipsum.
- Taglines are short and specific ("Nothing random.", "Your network. Our watch."), never corporate slogans.
- One dominant palette: base, one primary accent, one secondary accent, neutrals. The accent repeats across panels. No rainbow, no purple-blue AI glow.
- Art-directed imagery: cinematic landscapes, dusk skies, halftone, CRT, material texture. No stock people, office photos or cliche robots.
- Mockups are identity applications, not feature demos: browser bar, terminal, badge, card stack, input bar. No busy dashboards.

## Avoid
Random floating icons, startup gradients, overdesigned marks, meaningless blobs, messy collages, fake tiny UI, too many colours, cheap neon, template brand boards. Make the composition quieter, sharper and more intentional. The reusable prompt structure is in references/prompt-template.md.

## Checklist
- [ ] Strategy line written; core metaphor named
- [ ] Logo is one ownable mark, repeated consistently
- [ ] Grid chosen; panel roles assigned; rhythm varies
- [ ] One palette, accents repeated, no rainbow
- [ ] Text sparse and readable; no fake or placeholder copy
- [ ] No copied real-world logos
