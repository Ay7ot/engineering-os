# Launch Strategy

## When to use
- Planning a product launch, feature announcement or release strategy.
- Building a go-to-market plan, waitlist, beta or early-access programme.
- Preparing a Product Hunt or community announcement.

## Core idea
- The best companies launch repeatedly: every feature and update is a chance to capture attention. A strong launch gets the product into users' hands early, learns from real feedback, and compounds momentum over time.

## The ORB framework
- Owned channels, which you control (though not the audience): email list, blog, podcast, branded community, website. They get more effective over time, with no algorithms or pay-to-play. Start with 1-2: a blog if the industry lacks quality content, email if people want direct updates, a community if engagement matters.
- Rented channels, which give visibility without control: social platforms, app stores and marketplaces, video, forums. Pick 1-2 where your audience is active, use them to drive traffic to owned channels, and never rely on them alone. Rented channels give speed, not stability.
- Borrowed channels, someone else's audience: guest content, podcast interviews, newsletter features, collaborations, webinars, speaking, influencer partnerships. Be proactive: list the leaders your audience follows, pitch win-win collaborations, find audience overlap, and set up affiliate or referral incentives. Convert borrowed attention into owned relationships.

## Five phases
1. Internal: recruit early users one-on-one for free, collect feedback on usability gaps and missing features, and get the prototype good enough to demo.
2. Alpha: a landing page with an early-access signup form, announce the product exists, invite testers individually, and have a working production MVP.
3. Beta: work through the early-access list (some free, some paid), market teasers about the problems you solve, and recruit friends, investors and influencers to test and share. Optional: a coming-soon page or waitlist, a beta label in the app, email invites, and a toggle for experimental features.
4. Early access: leak details (screenshots, feature GIFs, demos), gather quantitative usage data and qualitative feedback, run research with engaged users, and optionally survey for product/market fit. Expand by throttling invites in 5-10% batches, or invite all users under an early-access framing.
5. Full launch: open self-serve signups, start charging, and announce general availability everywhere. Touchpoints: customer email, in-app popups and product tours, a site banner, a "new" label, a blog post, social posts, and directories such as Product Hunt, BetaList and Hacker News.

## Product Hunt
- Pros: exposure to an early-adopter audience, a credibility bump (especially Product of the Day), and possible press and backlinks. Cons: very competitive ranking, short-lived traffic spikes, and significant preparation.
- Before: build relationships with supporters, content hubs and communities; optimise the listing (tagline, visuals, short demo video); study successful launches; give value in communities before pitching; prepare the team for all-day engagement.
- On the day: treat it as an all-day event, reply to every comment in real time, answer questions and spark discussion, rally your existing audience, and route traffic to your site to capture signups.
- After: follow up with everyone who engaged, convert the traffic into owned relationships such as email signups, and continue with post-launch content.

## Post-launch and ongoing
- Educate new users with an automated onboarding sequence; repeat the announcement in the next roundup email; publish comparison pages; update site pages for the new feature; and offer an interactive no-code demo.
- Prioritise announcements: major updates (new features, overhauls) get a full multi-channel campaign; medium updates (integrations, UI work) get a targeted email and in-app banner; minor updates (fixes, tweaks) get a changelog entry only.
- Stagger releases to sustain momentum, reuse tactics that resonated, and keep signalling active development even with small changelog updates.

## Checklist
- [ ] Landing page with a clear value proposition
- [ ] Email capture or waitlist live; early-access list built
- [ ] Owned channels established and rented profiles optimised
- [ ] Borrowed opportunities identified (podcasts, influencers)
- [ ] Launch assets ready (screenshots, demo video, GIFs)
- [ ] Onboarding flow and analytics or tracking in place
- [ ] Launch-day runbook: email, blog, social, in-app, team on standby
- [ ] Post-launch: onboarding sequence, follow-ups, roundup, next launch planned
