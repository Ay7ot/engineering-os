# Guest insights on usability testing

Sources for the principles in the `usability-testing` skill. 11 guests, 14
insights.

## Amjad Masad
- **Insight:** AI tools let PMs bypass the engineering bottleneck and build a
  functional v1 for real user testing.
- **Advice:** build a functional v1 independently to validate with users before
  putting the idea on the official engineering roadmap.

## Bob Baxley
- **Insight:** product intuition comes from watching humans interact with
  technology in the wild, outside controlled lab settings.
- **Advice:** run "reality checks" by watching users interact with competitor
  products, to see unbiased behaviour and needs.

## Grant Lee
- **Insight:** rapid prototyping plus unbiased user testing can surface friction
  within a single day.
- **Advice:** recruit prospective users with zero skin in the game for honest
  feedback; watch users struggle and hear their thought process in real time; use
  platforms that automate and scale recruitment.

## Guillermo Rauch
- **Insight:** directly observing users overcomes creator bias, the tendency to
  overrate how well your own product works.
- **Advice:** invite customers to demo how they use the product live to the team;
  watch for pain points and non-intuitive behaviour that metrics miss.

## Itamar Gilad
- **Insight:** faked versions of a product (Wizard of Oz, smoke tests) validate
  the core value proposition before production code is written.
- **Advice:** run Wizard of Oz tests where humans manually perform the automated
  task behind a facade; use fake door tests to measure intent and click-through
  on features that do not exist yet.
- **Insight:** confidence grows through a progression of testing stages.
- **Advice:** move from fish fooding (testing on your own team) to dogfooding
  (company) to alphas and longitudinal studies, before a full launch.

## Judd Antin
- **Insight:** micro-level evaluative research and usability testing produce
  large business value through small tactical optimisations. Changing a button's
  text by seven characters made a large difference because the button felt scary.
- **Advice:** do not dismiss usability testing as junior work; look for scary or
  confusing calls to action blocking the conversion funnel.
- **Insight:** dogfooding finds candidate issues, but external user testing is
  needed to prioritise them, because employees are not like users.
- **Advice:** use dogfooding to build the issue list, then use research to
  prioritise; acknowledge that internal intuition is biased by product knowledge.

## Kristen Berman
- **Insight:** testing a single design cannot predict behaviour, since users may
  like or dislike every option equally.
- **Advice:** always present multiple design variations and compare which one
  drives the target behaviour.

## Melanie Perkins
- **Insight:** small, frequent tests with random participants are surprisingly
  representative of broader user friction.
- **Advice:** run tests with as few as 10 random people to find core product
  issues; online platforms bring frank feedback from users in their own
  environment.

## Noah Weiss
- **Insight:** having a cross-functional team react live to sessions increases
  engagement with research.
- **Advice:** create a dedicated thread per live session and encourage engineers
  and designers to share real-time observations and pain points.

## Upasna Gautam
- **Insight:** in high-pressure environments users will always prioritise the
  live event over a research session, so scheduling must be flexible.
- **Advice:** build in buffers and backups for sessions with busy stakeholders
  and be ready to pivot or reschedule at short notice.
- **Insight:** simulated dress rehearsals effectively test performance and
  workflows under high-stress, time-sensitive conditions.
- **Advice:** script realistic scenarios for users to play out in the product,
  with engineers and support observing in real time to find friction points.

## Uri Levine
- **Insight:** direct observation reveals behaviours and needs that founders
  miss because they are not representative users.
- **Advice:** watch new users without intervening, and ask why whenever a user
  deviates from the expected path.
