# Content Strategy

## When to use
- Choosing what content to create next, or building a content plan from scratch.
- Defining content pillars and topic clusters.
- Finding topics from customer conversations, surveys, forums or competitor gaps.
- Reprioritising an existing editorial calendar.

## Before planning
- Read the project marketing context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`) first, then ask only what is missing.
- Business: what the company does, the ideal customer, the primary goal (traffic, leads, awareness, thought leadership), and the problems the product solves.
- Customer research: pre-purchase questions, sales objections, recurring support tickets, and the language customers actually use.
- Current state: what content exists and performs, plus the writers, budget and formats available.
- Competitive landscape: main competitors and the gaps in their coverage.

## Searchable vs shareable
- Every piece must be searchable, shareable, or both. Prioritise in that order: search traffic is the foundation.
- Searchable content captures existing demand: target one query, match intent exactly, mirror query language in titles and headings, cover the topic fully, and cite data and sources.
- Shareable content creates demand: lead with a novel insight or original data, challenge conventional wisdom with evidence, tell true stories, and connect to current trends.
- Optimise both for AI discovery: clear positioning, structured content, and consistent brand naming across the web (`ai-seo`).

## Pillars and clusters
- Pick 3-5 pillars the brand will own. Identify them from the product (problems solved), the audience (what they must learn), search volume, or competitor coverage.
- Each pillar spawns a cluster of related pieces. Keep most content under `/blog` with strong internal links; reserve dedicated hub URLs (for example `/guides/topic`) for topics with genuine layered depth.

## Match topics to buyer stage
- Awareness: "what is", "how to", "guide to", "introduction to".
- Consideration: "best", "top", "vs", "alternatives", "comparison".
- Decision: "pricing", "reviews", "demo", "trial", "buy".
- Implementation: "templates", "examples", "tutorial", "how to use", "setup".

## Where ideas come from
- Keyword exports (group into clusters, tag stage and intent, flag quick wins and gaps).
- Sales and support calls (questions, pain, objections, exact phrasing, competitor mentions).
- Surveys (themes mentioned by 30 percent or more, resource requests, format preferences).
- Forums and communities (top posts, frustrations, upvoted answers).
- Competitor content (top performers, repeated topics, gaps, outdated pages).
- Customer-facing teams (objections, repeated questions, ticket patterns, success stories).
- Method detail and search operators: `references/ideation-sources.md`.

## Prioritise
Score each idea, then rank:
- Customer impact (40 percent): how often the topic appeared, how many customers face it, how charged it is, and the value of customers with that need.
- Content-market fit (30 percent): whether it aligns with the product and supports unique insight or customer stories.
- Search potential (20 percent): volume, competitiveness, long-tail options, and trend direction.
- Resources (10 percent): available expertise, extra research, and assets needed.

## Output
- 3-5 content pillars with rationale, subtopics, and the link to the product.
- A prioritised topic list, each with title, searchable or shareable, content type, target keyword, buyer stage, and the research backing it.
- A topic cluster map showing how pieces interlink.

## Checklist
- [ ] Marketing context read; gaps confirmed with the user
- [ ] Primary goal named (traffic, leads, awareness, authority)
- [ ] 3-5 pillars chosen with subtopics
- [ ] Topics tagged by buyer stage and search intent
- [ ] Ideas sourced from at least two research inputs
- [ ] Ideas scored and ranked with the four factors
- [ ] Output includes pillars, priority topics and a cluster map
