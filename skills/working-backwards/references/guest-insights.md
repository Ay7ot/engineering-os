# Working Backwards: insights from product leaders

Distilled from interviews with product leaders who ran the working backwards
process. Use these to diagnose which step a team skipped.

## Start with the customer problem

"Working backwards is all about the problem and starting there. Teams that do it
wrong do not work backwards, they have something they want to build." The
failure mode is retrofitting a problem to a solution. Write the problem
paragraph first, in the customer's words.

## Trust that results follow value

"Think about serving the customer well and trust that things like revenue and
share price would follow." Work backwards from the ideal customer solution
rather than forwards from the metric you are told to move.

## Use backcasting to escape the present

"Standing in that future, looking back to the present, what did we do to make
this future happen? That is how you unshackle yourself from the past." Envision
a radically different future, then work backwards, without letting current
constraints set the ceiling.

## The PR/FAQ forces clarity

"Start by writing a press release describing the feature in a way that speaks to
the customer. The idea better jump off the page as something a customer will
really need." A problem paragraph, a solution paragraph, a customer quote, and a
leadership quote are written before anything is built.

## Evaluate three things

"One, is it a big idea? Two, is it something we should be doing? Three, is there
a legitimate plan to succeed?" A proposal must pass all three. The FAQ is what
proves whether a legitimate plan exists.

## Define the ideal state first

"Work from this dream reality and work backwards from how to achieve that."
Picture the mythical perfect experience first, then build the rungs of a ladder
from today to that vision.

## Align the whole machinery

"Work backwards from an entire machinery at a particular day that is working.
What machinery would we have had to create for this product to succeed?" Working
backwards includes go-to-market, organisational design, and stakeholder buy-in,
not just the product.

## Define the theoretical ideal

"What is the theoretical minimum cost? The theoretical maximum speed? Not just
make it instant and cheap, but what is the lowest it could possibly be?" Define
the best possible outcome and work back from it, not from an incremental
improvement on the status quo.
