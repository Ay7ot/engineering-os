# Founder Sales

## When to use
- Closing the first handful of customers and doing founder-led sales.
- Writing early outreach to a named list, or preparing a first discovery call.
- Deciding whether to hire a salesperson yet.
- Deals stall and it is unclear whether the problem is outreach, discovery, closing or implementation.

## Diagnose first
Ask how many customers have been closed personally, whether an ICP is written
down, and where deals actually die. Then work on that stage only. Treat early
calls as research sessions, not just revenue.

## Principles
- The founder is the product. In early sales the founder's insight and expertise are what create interest. Use founder status for market access.
- Your competitor is indecision, not another vendor. Roughly 40 to 60 percent of B2B purchase processes end in no decision. Help the buyer choose confidently; be their guide through the market.
- You cannot outsource the first sales. Become minimally viable at selling and close the first couple of dozen customers yourself before considering a VP of Sales.
- Sell it before you build it. Mock-ups and concierge setups can secure the first 5 to 10 customers. Pick the demo fidelity that fits the product.
- Diagnose before demoing. In early meetings keep the laptop closed and ask whether the problem is real for them. Capture their exact words, on paper if that helps.
- Do the "Collison install". A deal is not done until the product is actually implemented. Stay through the last mile, even installing it yourself.
- Qualify ruthlessly. It is never a bottom-of-funnel problem, it is qualification. Check whether they measure or manage the problem today. "Sounds interesting" is not intent.
- Manual outreach beats database-driven automation for high-value deals. Everyone draws from the same lists. Take the back door in and keep emails to three or four sentences. See `cold-email`.
- Book the next meeting while still on this one. Pull up calendars, set the follow-up, and invite the other stakeholders.
- Design the process around how they buy, not how you sell. Map their timeline: first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, first use, ongoing use.

## Stay founder-led until it is repeatable
Hand over only once the motion repeats: a defined ICP, documented discovery
questions, objection handling and demo flow that work, typically around a
million in ARR.

## Questions to ask
- "How many customers have you closed personally? What is your ICP?"
- "Where are you losing deals: outreach, discovery, closing or implementation?"
- "Are you talking to people actively solving this, or just interested?"
- "What happened in the last deal you lost?"
- "What does your outreach say, and what response rate does it get?"
- "Have you documented what works so someone else could run it?"

## Common mistakes
- Hiring sales too early to fix messaging that has not landed.
- Defaulting to a product demo when discovery should focus on the customer's pain.
- Treating "yes" as the finish line and dropping implementation.
- Using generic automated outreach for high-value deals.
- Chasing polite leads with no active intent.

## Checklist
- [ ] Bottleneck identified before tactics
- [ ] Outreach to a named list, three or four sentences, manually personalised
- [ ] Discovery diagnostic, laptop closed, customer words captured
- [ ] Qualification checks for a live, measured problem
- [ ] Next meeting booked during the current call
- [ ] What worked documented for eventual handover
