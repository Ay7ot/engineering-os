# Lead Magnets

## When to use
- Choosing what to offer in exchange for an email address.
- Designing a gated asset, its delivery and its promotion.
- A lead magnet exists but captures poorly or attracts the wrong leads.

## Before planning
- Read the project marketing context document (for example `docs/marketing-context.md`) first.
- Business: what the company does, the ideal customer, and the problems the product solves.
- Current lead generation: how leads are captured now, what offers exist, and the current capture conversion rate.
- Existing assets: posts, guides or data that could be repurposed, plus internal templates or tools worth packaging.
- Goal: list growth, lead quality, or product education; the target buyer stage; and the time and budget available.

## Principles
1. Solve one specific problem, not a broad topic. "How to write cold emails that get replies" beats "marketing guide".
2. Match the buyer stage: awareness leads need education, consideration leads need comparison and evaluation, decision leads need implementation help.
3. High perceived value, low time investment: worth paying for, consumable in under 30 minutes (ideally under 10), with an immediate takeaway.
4. Natural path to product: solves a problem the product also solves and shows where the product fits.
5. Easy to consume: one format, works on mobile, no special software.

## Formats
Common formats and their effort: checklist (1-2 hours), cheat sheet (2-4), template for a document, spreadsheet or workspace (2-8), swipe file (4-8), ebook or guide (1-3 weeks), email mini-course (1-2 weeks), video mini-course (2-4 weeks), quiz or assessment (1-2 weeks), webinar (about a week of prep), resource library and free trial or community access (ongoing).
- Format detail, stage matching and benchmarks: `references/formats.md`.

## Gating
- Full gate: maximum capture, lower reach; suits high-value, bottom-of-funnel assets.
- Partial gate: preview plus full version; balances reach and capture.
- Ungated with an optional signup: maximum reach, lowest capture; suits top-of-funnel education.
- Content upgrade: a bonus specific to one post; converts 2-5 times better than a generic sidebar offer.
- Ask for the minimum. Email only converts best; email plus name adds personalisation at a small cost; company and role improve qualification; multi-field forms only suit high-value offers such as webinars and demos. Each extra field costs roughly 5-10 percent conversion.
- Frame the exchange: state the value, show a preview (table of contents, first page, sample results), add social proof such as a download count, and reduce risk with "no spam, unsubscribe anytime".

## Landing page and delivery
Page structure: headline with the benefit, a visual preview of the asset, 3-5 "what is inside" points, social proof, a short form with a clear button, and an FAQ covering hesitations. Optimise the page with `page-cro`.

Delivery options: instant download (immediate, no verification), email delivery (verifies the address and starts the relationship), thank-you page plus email (best of both), and drip delivery for courses or series.

Do not waste the thank-you page. Confirm delivery, offer a next step such as a demo, trial or community, make sharing easy with pre-written text, and recommend related content.

## Distribution
- In-post CTAs and post-specific content upgrades.
- Exit-intent and scroll-triggered popups matched to the page content.
- Social teasers, carousels of key points, and the magnet as the profile CTA (`social`).
- Paid amplification: broad-reach lead ads for top-funnel assets, search for high-intent assets, professional networks for B2B, and retargeting of blog visitors (`ads`).
- Partner co-promotion: cross-promote with complementary brands, run joint webinars, and appear in partner newsletters.

## Measure
- Landing page conversion rate: roughly 20-40 percent on warm traffic and 5-15 percent on cold.
- Cost per lead, by channel.
- Lead-to-customer rate: often 1-5 percent in B2B.
- Email engagement after delivery: roughly 30-50 percent open and 2-5 percent click.
- Lead quality signals: above-average engagement, normal trial or demo progression, low unsubscribe rate, and a match to the ideal customer profile.

Test one variable at a time (headline framing, format, gate level, form fields, button copy, delivery method) using `ab-testing`.

## Output
- A recommendation: format, topic, buyer stage, why it suits this audience, and estimated effort.
- A content outline: sections, scope, and what makes it unique.
- A gating and capture plan: what to gate, which fields, and the page structure.
- A distribution plan: channels, content upgrades, and paid amplification if relevant.
- A measurement plan: KPIs, targets, and the first test to run.

## Checklist
- [ ] Marketing context read; goal and buyer stage named
- [ ] One specific problem chosen, not a broad topic
- [ ] Format fits the stage and the time budget
- [ ] Gate level and form fields justified
- [ ] Landing page has preview, "what is inside", proof and FAQ
- [ ] Delivery method chosen and thank-you page used
- [ ] Distribution plan covers owned and paid channels
- [ ] KPIs and first test defined before launch
