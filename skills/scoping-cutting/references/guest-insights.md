# Scoping and cutting: insights from product leaders

Distilled from interviews with product leaders on defining MVPs and cutting
scope. Use these to name the mistake a project is making.

## Use appetite, not estimates

"We go the other way around and say, what is the maximum amount of time we are
willing to go before we actually finish something?" Set a fixed time budget and
design a version of the solution that fits it. Vary scope, never the deadline.

## An MVP is a validation tool

"For whatever the hypothesis is that we are trying to test, what is the most
efficient way to get the validation we need?" An MVP is not a low-quality
product. It is the cheapest sufficient test of one specific belief.

## Cut the list in half, then half again

"Write out the list of features that are necessary in your MVP. Cut it in half
and cut it in half again and build that." Founders consistently overestimate
what counts as minimum. Only aggressive cutting reaches a true baseline.

## Fixed time, small teams

"Our appetite for any individual feature is no more than six weeks. So we have
to figure out the simplest, most effective version to get that done within six
weeks, and get it done by two people." Constraints force creative solutions.
Limit the team size to protect focus.

## Build the scooter, not the axle

"If you are trying to build the minimum viable product for a car, do not build
just the wheels and the axle, build the scooter first." An MVP should be a
functional, end-to-end version of a smaller value proposition.

## Use a supplied-human test

"Coordinators acted as the product behind the scenes and we validated the value
proposition without building anything." Validate value manually before
investing in engineering. A person behind the curtain can simulate automation.

## Kill projects that do not finish in time

"If there is work left over and we do not know how we are going to do it, and we
are at our time limit, it almost certainly dies." Let projects die at the
appetite limit rather than extending indefinitely.

## Build the option to cut into the process

"We added into our process a notion that we might pivot or cut stuff we put on
our roadmap, because it felt like once it was on the roadmap it had to be done."
Make cutting and pivoting a formal option so sunk cost does not carry the work.
